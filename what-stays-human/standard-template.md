# How we use AI: what stays human

_Owner: <role> · Last updated: <date> · Register: <link>_

This page says how our team decides when AI does a step and when a person does. It isn't a policy
and it isn't legal advice. It tells us how much review each use gets, and when to ask a specialist.

## The rule

A step stays human if it needs **any one** of these:

- **Judgment:** a trade-off no rule settles, where someone has to own the call.
- **Relationship:** someone on the other end expects a person.
- **Legal standing:** a signature, consent, a license, or someone who can be held liable.
- **Voice and strategy:** it says what we stand for, or sets direction.

Everything else can be automated, with the review level below. Approve and review steps loosen as a
use earns trust. Relationship steps never do.

## How much review

| Level | What happens | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| 3 · Specialist review | Cleared before use | <e.g. outside counsel for rights and likeness; IT lead for data> |
| 2 · Sign-off | Every output approved before it leaves the team | The owner role |
| 1 · Spot check | Outputs sampled on a schedule | The owner role, <how often> |
| 0 · Logged | In the register, no routine review | Nobody, unless something goes wrong |

A new use starts at the highest level its data, its audience and our hard lines call for. A clean
record of **<four weeks or ten runs>** earns one level less at the next check-in. Any problem moves
it back up at once.

## How to ask

Before you start using AI for something new, log it: say what it would do, in which tool, with what
data, and where the output goes. You'll get a review level and the steps that stay human. Nothing
starts until the owner says yes.
