# What Stays Human

One register for every way your team uses AI, with an owner for each use and the right amount of
review. Review starts high and loosens as a use earns trust. A few steps stay human no matter what.

**You can't steer what you can't see.**

It's built for whoever runs operations for a small team or a team inside a larger company: a
producer, a head of operations, a studio manager. It isn't an AI policy, and it isn't legal advice.
It tells you which uses need a lawyer or a specialist to look, and it keeps the record.

## What it does

| Mode | When you use it | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **setup** | Once, at the start | Your one-page standard: the four bars, your review levels, your hard lines, who owns what |
| **log** | Someone wants to start using AI for something | One register row, a starting review level, and the steps that stay human |
| **sweep** | Once at the start, then whenever you suspect drift | Proposed rows for everything already in use, with the gaps first |
| **check-in** | Monthly or quarterly | Review levels moved up or down from the record, and a short read-out: what to buy, what guidance to write, who should talk, what leadership can say |

## The idea in three parts

1. **The four bars.** A step stays human if it needs judgment, a relationship, legal standing, or
   voice and strategy. Everything else is a candidate for automation.
2. **The review dial.** Every use gets a level from 3 (specialist review) to 0 (logged). A clean
   record earns one level less at each check-in, down to a floor you set. A problem moves it back
   up at once.
3. **One register.** Every use, its owner role, its data and its review level, in one place. Once
   it's all in one place, it tells you what to buy, what guidance to write, and who should be
   talking to whom.

## Install

1. Download this folder.
2. Add it as a skill in your AI tool (in Claude: Settings → Capabilities → Skills, or drop the
   folder into your skills directory).
3. Say "set up our AI register". It reads anything you link first, then asks only what it still
   needs.

The register can live in any sheet, tracker or database. A CSV is fine to start.
